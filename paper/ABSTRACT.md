# Physics-Informed Hybrid Hamiltonian Neural Network and Graph Neural Network for Industrial Heat Pump Coefficient of Performance Prediction

## Abstract

Accurate prediction of the Coefficient of Performance (COP) for industrial heat pump systems remains a significant challenge due to the complex interplay of thermodynamic cycles, component degradation, environmental conditions, and control system dynamics. Traditional physics-based models require extensive computational resources and detailed system parameters, while purely data-driven approaches often fail to respect fundamental thermodynamic constraints such as the Carnot limit. This work presents a hybrid approach that integrates comprehensive physics-based modeling with a novel Hamiltonian Neural Network and Graph Neural Network (HNN-GNN) architecture for COP prediction.

We develop a modular physics simulation framework comprising 17 interconnected physics models that capture the complete thermodynamic behavior of vapor compression heat pump systems. These models include: (1) refrigerant thermodynamic properties computed via CoolProp for R410A, R134a, and R290 refrigerants; (2) heat exchanger fouling degradation models that track thermal resistance accumulation over operational lifetime; (3) compressor mechanical wear models accounting for bearing degradation, valve leakage, and oil quality deterioration; (4) refrigerant charge deviation models that relate subcooling, superheat, and COP to charge level; (5) fault injection models for simulating refrigerant leaks and compressor degradation scenarios; (6) ambient condition models incorporating wet bulb temperature, air density, and frost formation risk calculations; (7) multi-zone building load models for thermal demand estimation; (8) Hamiltonian mechanics models that enforce energy conservation through symplectic integration; (9) control system dynamics models with PID controller simulation for compressor speed, expansion valve position, and fan speed modulation; (10) component interaction models capturing thermal and mass coupling between heat exchangers; and (11) part-load cycling models based on AHRI degradation curves for single-speed, two-speed, variable-speed, and hot-gas bypass modulation strategies. Each cycle generated by the framework undergoes rigorous validation including Carnot limit enforcement, energy balance verification, and entropy generation checks to ensure physical plausibility.

The neural network architecture processes 2,832 physics-derived features per heat pump operational cycle. The Hamiltonian Neural Network component receives temporal trajectories of 100 timesteps across 23 channels, including evaporator and condenser temperatures, pressures, mass flow rates, heat transfer rates, superheat, subcooling, control signals, and Hamiltonian phase space coordinates. The HNN learns energy-conserving dynamics by parameterizing the system Hamiltonian and computing time derivatives through automatic differentiation, thereby embedding the principle of energy conservation directly into the network structure. The Graph Neural Network component models the heat pump as a heterogeneous graph with 10 nodes representing physical components (compressor, condenser, evaporator, expansion valve, refrigerant circuits, control system, ambient environment, building load, and system health), connected by 11 edges encoding mass and thermal coupling relationships. Node features capture component-specific state variables including temperatures, pressures, efficiencies, degradation levels, and control setpoints, while 50 graph-level features encode system-wide operating conditions. Message passing layers propagate information across the component graph, enabling the model to learn inter-component dependencies that are critical for accurate COP estimation.

The physics models generate training data through a stratified sampling approach that ensures coverage of diverse operating conditions including ambient temperatures from -20°C to +45°C, part-load ratios from 30% to 100%, system ages from 0 to 50,000 operating hours, and refrigerant charge levels from 70% to 130% of nominal.
